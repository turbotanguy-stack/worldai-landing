### Lesson: AI Influencers Explained

**Slide 1 — Title:** AI INFLUENCERS EXPLAINED

**Slide 2 — What Is an AI Influencer?**
- AI influencers are virtual characters created entirely through AI technologies such as Nano Banana and other generative models
- They are designed to look, act, and interact like real people, producing content that feels authentic and relatable
- Today's AI is so sophisticated that 99% of viewers cannot reliably distinguish between what is real and what is fake
- This allows creators to build fully developed personas with a consistent identity — including their face, personality, and voice — that syncs videos, lifestyle posts, and high-impact content that sparks engagement and builds a loyal following
- Strategy: capture attention first, then guide that attention toward monetization
- Primary monetization platforms: Throne, where fans send gifts and tips; and Fanvue, which offers subscriptions, paid content, and private chat — this is where the real revenue comes from: subscriptions, tips, gifts, and premium content sales

**Slide 3 — Is This Legal?**
- Yes, as long as it is done the right way
- The course teaches how to run a fully compliant, transparent AI influencer business
- "Done the right way" means:
  - You are not pretending to be a real person; your AI character is a clearly fictional entity with its own identity, style, and backstory
  - You follow each platform's rules on AI content and disclosure
  - You know exactly what must be stated, what to avoid, and how to stay compliant on TikTok, Instagram, YouTube, and monetization platforms
  - You use the right tools to generate content, metadata, and watermarks when needed, so everything is traceable and aligned with platform policies
  - You never engage in illegal activity — no impersonation, fraud, harassment, or deceptive financial schemes
  - You create entertainment or inspirational content and monetize attention ethically — by being upfront and honest with your audience

**Slide 4 — The Three Parts of the Business**
Every successful AI influencer business is built on three essential components that work in sync:
1. **Content that grows reach and engagement** — This is the engine. The algorithm rewards value and holds attention. Without strong, high-quality content, nothing else has the chance to work.
2. **Character that builds connection and trust** — Consistency creates recognition. Recognition builds trust. Trust drives loyalty — and loyalty drives engagement and long-term growth.
3. **Conversion funnel that turns attention into revenue** — Followers don't just pay — systems do. A solid funnel moves people from interest to transaction through clear pathways: bio links, CTAs, captions, and content designed for conversion across platforms like Throne, Fanvue, and other monetization channels.

All three parts must work together. If one is weak, the whole system suffers:
- Strong content without a funnel = lots of followers, no income
- Strong funnel without an audience = nothing to convert
- Weak character without a pipeline = people leave when inconsistencies show
- A repeatable workflow builds a personal brand that scales

### Lesson: Execution Order

**Slide 1 — Title:** EXECUTION ORDER

**Slide 2 (22.png) — The Content Creation Workflow**
Every piece of AI influencer content follows the same repeatable process:

**Ideation → Prompt Creation → Content Generation → Cleaning → Posting**

- Each step plays a specific role in the production pipeline:
  - Finding the idea
  - Creating the prompt
  - Generating the content
  - Removing AI traces and polishing the result
  - Publishing the final content
- *"This is the exact workflow you'll repeat hundreds of times."*
- Once mastered, content production becomes: faster; more consistent; easier to scale; higher quality overall
- The next sections break down each step individually

**Slide 3 (23.png) — Step 1: Ideation**
Before creating content, you should already know the platform and format and what result you want from it:
- **Define the platform & format:** start by selecting the platform. TikTok, Instagram, Snapchat, and YouTube all have different audiences, formats, and content styles. Then decide the format: reel; story; feed post; carousel
- **Identify the main goal:** identify the main purpose of the post: growing the audience; following trends; promoting the character through comments, replies, or shares
- **Niche & Identity fit:** each way performs differently and requires a different structure. You also need to make sure the content fits your niche and character identity — content that doesn't match the page usually weakens engagement and confuses followers
- The ideation phase is more strategy than creative. Strong content is intentional — every post should have a clear role in the overall brand and growth strategy

**Slide 4 (24.png) — Step 2: Prompt Creation**
- The prompt is what you feed to AI to generate images or videos — the more detailed and precise the prompt, the better and more consistent the final result will be
- **Strong prompts usually include:** appearance details such as hairstyle, clothing, environment; camera style; pose and overall vibe
- **Example — Bad prompt:** "girl in gym"
- **Better prompt:** "upper body mirror selfie of a young athletic blonde woman wearing black gymwear, standing in a modern luxury gym, soft lighting, iPhone selfie style, realistic skin texture, fitness influencer aesthetic"
- **Consistency & tips:**
  - Always prompt in a consistent style aligned with your character's identity
  - Similar age; similar vibe; recognizable body type; consistent aesthetic
  - Describe what you want directly instead of using negative wording. E.g. "Empty gym" works better than "No people in the gym."
- As you create more content, you'll slowly build a collection of preset prompts that can be reused and adapted instead of creating strong ones from scratch each time

**Slide 5 (25.png) — Step 3: Content Generation**
- Don't rush the generation phase — poor quality at this stage is impossible to fix, no matter how much editing is added later
- **When generating content:**
  - Never settle for the first result. Generate several images or videos and choose the strongest one. More variations increase the chances of getting a high-quality output
  - **Carefully inspect the details:** zoom into generated images; check elements such as: hands or fingers; unrealistic text or fonts; unrealistic eyes or facial details; duplicate limbs or floating objects; inconsistent lighting; attention to detail saves editing time later
  - **Regenerate instead of over-editing:** if something looks wrong, it's usually faster and cleaner to regenerate the content with a better prompt rather than trying to fully fix it in editing
  - **Save your best generations:** when you get a strong result, keep the prompt and settings used. Over time, this becomes a personal library of proven generations you can never lose

**Slide 6 (26.png) — Step 4: Cleaning**
- Social media platforms are becoming increasingly effective at detecting AI-generated content
- Before posting, it's important to remove metadata, watermarks, and other detectable AI traces to reduce the chances of triggering platform detection systems
- **Platforms like Instagram, Telegram, and TikTok can scan for:**
  - EXIF and generation metadata
  - Visible or hidden watermarks
  - SynthID and other invisible AI markers
- **If too many AI traces are detected, content may:**
  - Receive AI-generated labels
  - Lose reach in the algorithm
  - Get shadowbanned or suppressed
  - Be flagged for review or restrictions
- Cleaning tools help the content appear more natural and reduce obvious indicators of AI generation. For many creators, this step is considered essential before uploading any content publicly

**Slide 7 (27.png) — Step 5: Posting**
- Once you have clean content, the goal is to behave as naturally as possible and avoid looking automated or spammy
- **Good posting practices:**
  - **Avoid mass-uploading content** — posting too many videos or images at once can look unusual and trigger platform detection systems; post more naturally: one or two pieces at a time

### Lesson: Prompting

**Slide 2 (42.png) — What Is Prompting?**
- Prompting is simply the process of giving instructions to an AI model so it can generate images or videos
- When using tools like Nano Banana or Seedream, you describe exactly what you want the AI to create — that written description is called a prompt
- **Example:** "selfie of a young woman with brown hair in a volleyball outfit in her bedroom"
- Even very simple prompts can already generate decent results and help you understand how prompting works before moving into more advanced prompting techniques later on

**Slide 3 (43.png) — What Makes a Good Prompt?**
- A prompt works best when the description follows a clean and logical order
- Most AI image models also understand prompts more accurately when they are written directly in English
- **Bad prompt:** "Athletic black woman, mirror selfie, white socks, black hoodie, ponytail, modern room, athletic body, messy bed, long blonde hair"
  - The bad prompt jumps randomly between different elements, causing the model to lose consistency
- **Good prompt:** "mirror selfie of a young athletic young woman with long blonde hair in a ponytail, wearing a black hoodie in a modern bedroom with/white walls and a slightly messy bed, realistic — *nice prompt*"
  - This version follows a much cleaner structure: the subject; the look and appearance; clothing; environment; accessories; camera style and overall vibe
- Organized prompts usually create: more realistic images; better consistency; fewer generation mistakes; cleaner compositions

**Slide 4 (44.png) — Negation**
- One common mistake in prompting is using too much negation — negation happens when you describe things using words like: "no"; "not"; "without"
- Many AI models respond better to direct positive descriptions rather than negative instructions, so using too much negation can confuse the generation
- **Bad Prompt:** "selfie of a young woman with pink hair wearing a hat without a logo and no heavy makeup"
- **Good Prompt:** "selfie of a young woman with pink hair wearing a plain hat and light natural makeup"
- Instead of describing what you don't want, it's usually more effective to directly describe what you do want
- **Another example:** Instead of "modern bedroom with no decorations" → "minimalistic modern bedroom"
- Cleaner and more direct prompts usually lead to: more accurate generations; fewer mistakes; and more consistent image quality

**Slide 5 (45.png) — JSON Prompting**
- JSON prompting is a more structured way of writing prompts that is easier for AI models to interpret and process consistently
- Unlike normal plain-language prompts, JSON formatting allows you to organize details into separate categories such as:
  - lighting
  - camera style
  - emotional tone
  - clothing
  - technical settings
- This gives much more control over the final output and often improves consistency across generations
- Supported by: Nano Banana; Seedream; and some other image generation models
- However, many video models — such as Kling — do not currently support this type of formatting
- At first, JSON prompting may look more technical or intimidating, but it becomes very useful once you want more precision and repeatable results in your generations

**Slide 6 (46.png) — AI Prompting**
- For most generations, using LLMs like Claude, ChatGPT, or Grok to help write prompts can save a huge amount of time
- Instead of manually composing every prompt from scratch, you can use AI to generate:
  - More detailed prompts
  - Better structure given to the AI
  - Consistent character details
  - Improved realism and scene descriptions
- This makes the workflow: faster; more scalable; more consistent across generations
- **Meta-Prompts:** many creators also build custom meta-prompts — reusable instruction sets given to the LLM to guide the way it generates prompts in a specific style or format. LLMs are especially useful because they can quickly turn simple ideas into high-quality prompts optimized for image generation models
- Over time, AI-assisted prompting becomes one of the most powerful ways to massively improve both prompt quality and content production speed

### Lesson: Telegram

**Source: TELEGRAM.pdf**

**Overview:**
- The best way to drive traffic to Telegram is by linking it on your Instagram stories — e.g., "Join my free telegram" or "Free community"
- It is a much lower barrier than Fanvue as Telegram is just seen as a normal social media app — trustworthy

**Method 1: Using Telegram as a Funnel**
- Create a public Telegram channel
- Add your AI's pic, and add your Fanvue or wherever you are trying to funnel traffic to in the bio
- Every day, send a pic of your model (or something off Pinterest) calling people to chat with you on Fanvue or to see more content
- You can get like 90 images off Pinterest, schedule them for every day and leave it alone

**Method 2: Selling Content on Telegram**
- You can also use the Telegram channel to sell content directly
- Use the "Make This Content Paid" option when sending a video file
- Users will have to buy Telegram Stars first — instead of directly buying the content
- This is the simplest way, but may have a lower conversion rate
- The second way is through Fanvue "Paid Media" links, or websites like **Dropp** — you can send a blurred image, then sell bundles or individual photos via a link attached in the caption

### Lesson: Throne

**Source: THRONE (1) (1).pdf**

**Overview:**
- Throne is recommended as a wish list/gifting platform
- It is better than CashApp/PayPal/Buy me a coffee as more of your details get shared
- Throne is especially good if you are in the entertainment niche

**Account Setup (step-by-step):**
1. Click "Get Started" and create an account — choose a simple name; note your social media username; people may have to search for you, so just keep it simple
2. Select your account type — select "I'm a creator"
3. Enter your shipping country — select YOUR country, NOT your AI's
4. Create your profile — mix a trial profile pic with your AI's face; choose a simple public name and not username
5. Link your Instagram or any other socials
6. What content do you create? — choose anything; it doesn't really matter; just go "other"

**Gift Setup (setting up items to receive):**
- You need to add gifts to your Throne profile to get automatically shipped to your address (some of your details and payment method will be required). Throne is especially good with the split gift option.
- **Cart Gift:** add a link to a real-life thing; it has to be a real thing; it can't just be an Amazon gift card or something. Add a photo (if needed) or a product link. Any person can buy the gift, so the creator gets the money.
- **Cash Gift:** you can also set up unconfirmed gifts. The money never touches the creator's account; instead, a person can choose the quantity to add, the amount of cash to it, and it is best to not receive more than the creator account is worth.
- **Surprise Gift Settings:** enable "Surprise Gifts Creator" — enabled gifts will be automatically generated for your profile based on products. Turn this off unless you want random stuff that you never asked for.

**Profile shown:**
- Example: Wishlist page for "mia" (@its_mia) showing items: "buy me a coffee" ($5.49), "buy me lunch" ($16.46), "get me a new gym set" ($49.38), "help me get a new phone" (0% funded, CrowdFunding/Contribute)

### Lesson: Amazon Wishlist

**Source: AMAZON WISHLIST (1).pdf**

**Overview:**
- Amazon wishlists offer creators a straightforward way to receive gifts from social media followers via direct purchases on Amazon
- They bypass subscription-based sites or +18 sites such as Fanvue if followers do not want that showing on their card statements
- Fans simply buy items from your public list, which ships to you without needing accounts elsewhere

**Step-by-step setup:**
1. **Login to Amazon** and click "Accounts & Lists"
2. **Scroll down** and click "Lists"
3. **Create a new list** — choose a name (can be changed); normally keep it simple and add the model's name. Note: depending on your region, "Lists" may be in a different place
4. **Click "Manage list"** — this is where you find your link to send to people/post
5. **Enter the relevant details:**
   - Set to **"Public"** so anyone can access your wishlist
   - Enter your model's name (this is the displayed name)
   - Add your email
   - Add delivery address
   - Turn **"Share my delivery address with sellers"** to **ON** — this means when people buy something off your list, sellers can ship it to you without the buyer seeing your address

### Core Workflow Summary
1. **Ideation** — Find inspiration on Pinterest, Instagram, TikTok
2. **Prompt Creation** — Use LLM + meta-prompt to generate structured JSON prompts
3. **Content Generation** — Generate images (Nano Banana / Seedream via Wavespeed), then animate with Kling 3.0
4. **Voice** — Add audio via ElevenLabs (Instant Speech or Voice Changer) or Kling native audio
5. **Cleaning** — Strip metadata, SynthID markers, and watermarks
6. **Editing** — Assemble in CapCut (captions, music, transitions)
7. **Posting** — Post natively via Instagram/TikTok app; vary timing; engage after posting
8. **Monetization** — Drive traffic via bio link to Throne / Fanvue / Telegram / Amazon Wishlist
